### Pill to treat pancreatic cancer hailed as 'biggest breakthrough in decades'
A new oral pill, Daraxonrasib, has been hailed as a groundbreaking treatment for pancreatic cancer, offering hope after decades of limited progress. Early trials show significant tumor reduction with fewer side effects compared to chemotherapy. Researchers call it the most promising advance since gemcitabine in the 1990s.

*Why it matters:* This could transform pancreatic cancer from a near-certain death sentence to a manageable chronic condition, saving thousands of lives annually.


### Toyota, Nissan, and Honda to standardize parts to lower costs
Japan's three largest automakers will collaborate to standardize vehicle components, aiming to reduce production costs by up to 30%. The initiative targets shared parts like sensors, batteries, and chassis components across their lineups. This follows similar moves by European automakers to compete with Tesla's vertical integration.

*Why it matters:* Standardization could accelerate EV adoption by lowering prices and simplifying supply chains for the entire industry.


### 87% of the Anthropic power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028
A new report reveals Anthropic's AI infrastructure will depend on 87% natural gas and 11% coal for power by 2028, despite sustainability pledges. The analysis highlights the growing energy demands of data centers supporting large language models. Renewables currently account for just 2% of their energy mix.

*Why it matters:* This underscores the urgent need for greener AI infrastructure as model sizes and energy consumption continue to explode.


### Inkling-2-Mini in 5.4 GB Memory
A new open-source model, Inkling-2-Mini, demonstrates that advanced AI reasoning can fit in just 5.4GB of memory. The model achieves performance comparable to larger systems while running efficiently on consumer-grade hardware. Developers highlight its potential for edge deployment and cost-effective inference.

*Why it matters:* This breakthrough could democratize AI by making powerful models accessible on low-cost devices without cloud dependencies.

### Perplexity and Intelligence
OpenAI explores the relationship between perplexity—a measure of model uncertainty—and intelligence in AI systems. The post argues that lower perplexity correlates with more reliable outputs but doesn’t guarantee true understanding. It calls for better metrics to evaluate AI capabilities beyond traditional benchmarks.

*Why it matters:* This could redefine how we measure AI progress, moving beyond accuracy scores to assess genuine reasoning and adaptability.


### Data Processing Incidents on Inference Providers
OpenRouter reports data policy violations by several AI inference providers, leading to the offboarding of three providers and removal of 125 endpoints. The incidents involved unauthorized data sharing or retention, despite claims of zero-data-retention policies. OpenRouter is implementing stricter verification processes.

*Why it matters:* This highlights critical trust and security gaps in the AI supply chain that could undermine user privacy and compliance.

### Open Social Web Alliance
The Open Social Web Alliance launches to promote decentralized, interoperable social media protocols. The initiative aims to counter platform monopolies by enabling users to own their data and switch between services seamlessly. Founding members include Mastodon and Matrix contributors.

*Why it matters:* This could restore competition and user control in social media, breaking the dominance of centralized platforms like Meta and X.


### Europe weakens chemical rules as science links them to 'lifestyle diseases'
Europe is rolling back chemical regulations despite mounting evidence linking certain substances to chronic diseases like diabetes and obesity. Critics argue the move prioritizes industry interests over public health, citing industry-funded studies that dispute the causal links. The policy shift affects REACH regulations.

*Why it matters:* This decision could have long-term health consequences and set a dangerous precedent for prioritizing economic growth over scientific consensus.


### Self-hosted library branch over public web OPDS

*Why it matters:* This empowers individuals and organizations to preserve and share books independently, bypassing commercial platforms.
