### My AI agents found an unmapped Maya site
An author used AI agents to analyze NASA lidar data and rediscover a Maya site in Mexico that wasn’t officially registered. The process took just 72 hours, showcasing the power of AI in archaeology. It later turned out the site had already been mapped, but the AI still uncovered a gap in public records.

*Why it matters:* This demonstrates how AI can accelerate discovery and highlight gaps in public datasets, even in fields like archaeology.


### Tim Peters has died
Tim Peters, a legendary Python contributor known for the Zen of Python and the Timsort algorithm, has passed away at age 74. The Python community mourns his loss, celebrating his technical brilliance and mentorship. Guido van Rossum shared the news, calling him irreplaceable.

*Why it matters:* Tim Peters’ contributions shaped Python’s design and culture, leaving a lasting legacy in the programming world.

### A false smoke alarm almost drowned Jane Street's only NYC datacenter

The systems team raced to unplug servers before water damage occurred, highlighting the fragility of datacenter infrastructure. The incident underscores the importance of fail-safes in critical systems.

*Why it matters:* This near-disaster reveals the hidden risks in datacenter operations and the need for robust contingency plans.

### Using voltage glitching to unleash the A100 in my mining card
A security researcher used voltage glitching to unlock A100-level performance in a CMP mining GPU. By exploiting hardware vulnerabilities, they enabled all functional streaming multiprocessors, boosting compute power by 20%. The technique also unlocked features like PCIe Gen4 and NVLink.

*Why it matters:* This highlights the risks of hardware-level vulnerabilities and the potential for creative exploitation in GPU performance tuning.


### Can a Linear Program Do Weight-Space Programming?
A researcher found that a linear program could replicate weight-space programming, a technique used to edit neural network weights without training loops. By solving an LP, they achieved similar results with fewer changes, improving efficiency. The work builds on MIT CSAIL’s recent breakthrough.

*Why it matters:* This could simplify and accelerate neural network optimization, reducing the need for computationally expensive training loops.
