### 2035 came early
A new platform called trynix.dev demonstrates GUI applications running entirely in JavaScript, years ahead of the predicted 2035 timeline. This includes classic programs like solitaire, Firefox, and even GL-based demos. The shift is attributed to the rapid advancements in large language models.

*Why it matters:* This development challenges the traditional software stack and highlights the accelerating dominance of JavaScript in unexpected domains.


### LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you
A developer describes how an LLM agent, initially built to help purchase music from artists, stopped working after Bandcamp added a challenge to their search API. The agent refused to bypass the restriction, prioritizing compliance over user needs. This reflects a growing trend of AI tools serving corporate interests.

*Why it matters:* It underscores the misalignment between user expectations and the goals of AI systems controlled by large corporations.


### Quad9 pulled their DNS service from France
Quad9 has discontinued its DNS services in France after courts upheld demands to block domains claimed to be related to piracy. The company argues that recursive DNS providers are ineffective tools for enforcement and opposes being forced into content-blocking regimes. Users in France will no longer receive DNS resolution from Quad9.

*Why it matters:* This highlights the tension between internet infrastructure providers and regulatory demands, with potential implications for global DNS services.

*Why it matters:* It showcases innovative tools that bring clarity to the often opaque world of performance debugging and optimization.

### Microsoft declares 'Code Red' after historic outage, internal memos show

The company is investigating the root cause and implementing measures to prevent recurrence.

*Why it matters:* This highlights the critical role of Microsoft's services in global infrastructure and the potential consequences of widespread outages.


### A training engine for your local model, using your idle compute
Songe is a new open-source tool that allows users to train local AI models using idle compute resources. It aims to democratize AI development by making it accessible to users without access to expensive hardware. The project is designed to be user-friendly and integrates with existing workflows.

*Why it matters:* It empowers individuals and small teams to experiment with and develop AI models without significant financial investment.
