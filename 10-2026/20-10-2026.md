### DeepInfer: High-performance, large-scale inference framework for DeepSeek models
DeepInfer is a new open-source framework designed to accelerate inference for DeepSeek models at scale. It leverages optimized kernels and distributed computing to handle large workloads efficiently. The project is available on GitHub and has already garnered significant attention.

*Why it matters:* DeepInfer could significantly reduce the cost and latency of running DeepSeek models, making advanced AI more accessible for developers and enterprises.


### WhatsApp's help pages hid LLM instructions: dont recommend a regulator
An investigation revealed that WhatsApp’s internal AI help pages instructed its LLM not to recommend regulators or government oversight. The hidden notes were discovered in the platform’s documentation and have raised concerns about transparency. The findings were published by Redact.dev.

*Why it matters:* This discovery highlights the lack of transparency in how major platforms deploy AI and the potential for hidden biases in user-facing systems.


### DeepSeek banned from U.S. federal civilian agencies under new DHS order

*Why it matters:* This ban reflects growing geopolitical tensions around AI and could set a precedent for other Western governments restricting foreign AI tools.

### OpenBSD 8.0
OpenBSD 8.0 has been released with major improvements across platforms, including expanded kernel address space on amd64, hibernate support for arm64, and AES-accelerated swap encryption. The update also retires support for the loongson architecture and introduces new drivers for RISC-V and SPARC64.

*Why it matters:* OpenBSD 8.0 continues the project’s commitment to security, performance, and platform diversity, making it a critical update for users prioritizing stability and safety.


### Apple broke an essential presentation feature of iPads
A regression in iPadOS 27 has broken a key feature for presenters, causing the external display to mirror the entire iPad screen when switching apps during AirPlay. This disrupts workflows for educators and professionals who rely on presenter views. The issue was highlighted in a blog post by Teddy Svoronos.

*Why it matters:* This bug undermines a core use case for iPads in education and business, forcing users to avoid critical updates or seek workarounds.

### MicroG: Free-libre re-implementation of Google's proprietary Android user space
MicroG provides an open-source alternative to Google’s proprietary Android services, enabling users to replace core Google components with free software. The project is documented on its GitHub wiki and aims to promote software freedom on Android devices.

*Why it matters:* MicroG offers a privacy-respecting and freedom-focused path for Android users who want to avoid Google’s ecosystem while maintaining functionality.


### EMudhra Intermediate CA Compromise
Eight intermediate CA certificates from EMudhra have been compromised, affecting trust stores across major platforms like Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple, and Chrome. The issue was responsibly disclosed and remediated, but users may still need to update their systems. A detailed breakdown is available on the project’s website.

*Why it matters:* This incident underscores the critical importance of certificate revocation and trust store updates in preventing widespread impersonation attacks.


### Publish fake stats, get cited in LLMs

*Why it matters:* This highlights a growing problem of data pollution in AI training datasets, which can distort model outputs and undermine trust in AI-generated content.

### How I got over 300 Tor exit nodes removed
A security researcher detailed how they identified and helped remove over 300 compromised Tor exit nodes that were intercepting SSH traffic and stealing credentials. The attack involved the Ebury backdoor and was coordinated across a significant portion of the Tor network.

*Why it matters:* This incident highlights the ongoing challenges of securing anonymity networks against determined attackers and the importance of rapid response in such cases.
